# Simulation Boundary Standard

## Purpose

This standard prevents the benchmark from being mistaken for or converted into real financial activity.

## Allowed

- Comparing publicly advertised broker, data, charting, scanner, and analytical offerings.
- Selecting a hypothetical provider and pricing tier.
- Modeling subscription, transaction, spread, slippage, data, and regulatory costs.
- Recording fictional positions and portfolio results.
- Evaluating public strategies, indicators, algorithms, academic research, and trading-bot methods.
- Producing benchmark reports and synthetic examples.

## Prohibited

- Opening, linking, funding, or authenticating a real account.
- Accepting contracts or terms of service.
- Purchasing or activating a paid product.
- Sending an order to a broker, exchange, prediction market, or wallet.
- Storing or using credentials capable of financial action.
- Recommending that a person copy a contender's fictional position.
- Describing a fictional action as if it happened in the real world.

## Required language

Operational records and reports must use clear simulation terms such as:

- fictional budget;
- hypothetical provider;
- simulated entry;
- modeled fee;
- paper-market position;
- benchmark result.

Avoid ambiguous claims such as `bought`, `sold`, `account opened`, or `subscription activated` without an explicit simulation qualifier.

## Escalation

If a workflow requests or appears capable of real financial action:

1. Stop the workflow.
2. Preserve the request and context as an incident record without secrets.
3. Notify the human maintainer.
4. Resume only after the capability has been removed or isolated.
