# Isolation and Fairness Standard

## Contender isolation

Each contender must have a unique isolation key and an authorized data scope.

A contender may access:

- shared project standards;
- its assigned cohort rules;
- its own configuration;
- its own journal entries, fictional ledger items, snapshots, and reports;
- public market information available before the run cutoff.

A contender may not access:

- another contender's unpublished symbols, watchlists, sources, hypotheses, fictional positions, costs, results, or reports;
- referee-only validation notes before scoring;
- future market information relative to its submission timestamp.

## Shared information

The following may be shared across contenders:

- cohort rules;
- platform-neutral prompts;
- execution and cost standards;
- scoring definitions;
- public data sources, provided each contender independently chooses and cites them;
- finalized public benchmark reports after the applicable scoring period closes.

## Late entrants

A contender that starts later must retain its actual calendar start date. Do not fabricate an earlier history.

Compare late entrants through:

1. active-day indexing;
2. matched-market windows;
3. benchmark-relative return over the contender's own dates;
4. separate cohort results when rules or market regimes differ materially.

## Model and prompt changes

A material model, prompt, tool, source-access, or cadence change creates a new phase. Prefer registering a new contender identity so results remain interpretable.

## Contamination incidents

Suspected cross-contender leakage must be recorded as an incident. The affected period is excluded from the normalized leaderboard until reviewed.
